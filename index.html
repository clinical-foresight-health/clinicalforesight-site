The premise

Important clinical information is buried in records that keep getting more complex.

Physicians make high-stakes decisions under real time pressure, across admission notes, histories, medication lists, labs, imaging, and specialist recommendations. Traditional CDI arrives afterward and reviews what was written. Clinical Foresight is built to work before the note is signed.

  • Reviews longitudinal clinical information, not isolated documentation or code matching.
  • Integrates patient safety and prospective CDI in a single physician-facing workflow.
  • Built around evidence-supported recommendations, explainable findings, and physician judgment.

Built around how hospitalists actually work

Three moments in the day. One continuous clinical review.

The review follows the physician through the hospitalization rather than arriving as a query days later.

01 · BEFORE ROUNDING

Review the record, surface what's easy to miss

The AI reads available admission documentation, histories, medications, labs, studies, and specialist notes. It flags potentially overlooked safety concerns and proposes high-value questions for the encounter.

02 · AFTER ROUNDING

Integrate what the physician learned

The physician adds updated history, exam findings, and decisions. The system folds that into its assessment and raises further questions or concerns where appropriate.

03 · BEFORE THE NOTE IS SIGNED

Combined safety and CDI review

A final pass identifies issues that warrant attention and opportunities to document supported diagnoses more precisely. The physician reviews, decides, and signs.

Product stage

Clinical Foresight is in prototype and integration development. A read-only Epic SMART on FHIR connection has retrieved synthetic sandbox patients; the physician-authored clinical engines are being integrated. The platform is not yet deployed in production hospital environments, and clinical effectiveness has not yet been validated.

How the products work together

Get documentation right the first time. Automate the rest.

Traditional CDI today

Review after the fact

  • Charts are read by hand, after documentation is complete.
  • Queries reach physicians days later, out of clinical context.
  • Safety and documentation are handled by separate teams and tools.
  • Cost scales with headcount; consistency depends on the reviewer.

With Clinical Foresight

Prospective clarity, then intelligent automation

  • The AI Hospitalist resolves documentation gaps while the physician is treating the patient.
  • Retrospective CDI screens what remains and prepares compliant queries.
  • One clinical evidence foundation serves both workflows.
  • Long-term: retrospective correction becomes increasingly unnecessary.

Why it's different
